Here are detailed notes covering the typical syllabus for 7th Standard (Grade) Mathematics, often based on NCERT or similar curricula. These notes aim to provide a clear understanding of concepts, key formulas, and important points for each chapter.

## 7th Standard Maths: Detailed Notes

### Chapter 1: Integers

*   **Definition:** Integers are a set of whole numbers and negative numbers. They include ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
    *   Positive Integers: 1, 2, 3, ...
    *   Negative Integers: -1, -2, -3, ...
    *   Zero: Neither positive nor negative.
*   **Number Line:** Integers can be represented on a number line. Numbers to the right are greater, and numbers to the left are smaller.
*   **Operations on Integers:**
    *   **Addition:**
        *   Same signs: Add the absolute values and keep the common sign. (e.g., 3 + 5 = 8; -3 + (-5) = -8)
        *   Different signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value and take the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. (e.g., -5 + 3 = -2; 5 + (-3) = 2)
    *   **Subtraction:** Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its additive inverse (opposite).
        *   a - b = a + (-b)
        *   a - (-b) = a + b
        *   (e.g., 5 - 3 = 2; 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8; -5 - 3 = -5 + (-3) = -8)
    *   **Multiplication & Division:**
        *   (+) × (+) = (+) ; (+) ÷ (+) = (+)
        *   (-) × (-) = (+) ; (-) ÷ (-) = (+)
        *   (+) × (-) = (-) ; (+) ÷ (-) = (-)
        *   (-) × (+) = (-) ; (-) ÷ (+) = (-)
        *   Product/Quotient of an even number of negative integers is positive.
        *   Product/Quotient of an odd number of negative integers is negative.
        *   Multiplication by 0: Any integer multiplied by 0 is 0.
        *   Division by 0: Division by 0 is undefined.
*   **Properties of Integers:**
    *   **Closure:** Integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication (a+b, a-b, a*b are always integers). Not closed under division.
    *   **Commutativity:**
        *   Addition: a + b = b + a
        *   Multiplication: a × b = b × a
        *   Not commutative for subtraction or division.
    *   **Associativity:**
        *   Addition: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
        *   Multiplication: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
        *   Not associative for subtraction or division.
    *   **Distributivity:** a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
    *   **Additive Identity:** a + 0 = a
    *   **Multiplicative Identity:** a × 1 = a
    *   **Additive Inverse:** a + (-a) = 0

### Chapter 2: Fractions and Decimals

*   **Fractions:** Parts of a whole.
    *   **Numerator:** Top number (number of parts taken).
    *   **Denominator:** Bottom number (total number of equal parts).
    *   **Types:**
        *   **Proper Fraction:** Numerator < Denominator (e.g., 1/2)
        *   **Improper Fraction:** Numerator ≥ Denominator (e.g., 5/3)
        *   **Mixed Fraction:** Whole number + Proper fraction (e.g., 1 2/3). Can convert to improper and vice versa.
        *   **Equivalent Fractions:** Represent the same value (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6).
    *   **Operations:**
        *   **Addition/Subtraction:** Requires a common denominator (LCM).
        *   **Multiplication:** (Numerator × Numerator) / (Denominator × Denominator).
        *   **Division:** Multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction (e.g., a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c).
*   **Decimals:** Numbers with a fractional part separated by a decimal point.
    *   **Place Value:** Hundreds, tens, ones . tenths, hundredths, thousandths...
    *   **Comparing Decimals:** Compare whole parts first, then tenths, hundredths, etc.
    *   **Operations:**
        *   **Addition/Subtraction:** Align decimal points vertically.
        *   **Multiplication:** Multiply as whole numbers, then count total decimal places in the factors and place the decimal point in the product.
        *   **Division:**
            *   By a whole number: Divide normally, place decimal point in quotient when dividing the fractional part.
            *   By a decimal number: Convert divisor to a whole number by multiplying both dividend and divisor by a power of 10. (e.g., 3.6 ÷ 0.2 = 36 ÷ 2).
*   **Conversions:**
    *   Fraction to Decimal: Divide numerator by denominator.
    *   Decimal to Fraction: Write as a fraction with denominator as a power of 10, then simplify (e.g., 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4).

### Chapter 3: Data Handling

*   **Data:** A collection of information (numbers, words, measurements, etc.).
*   **Raw Data:** Data collected in its original form.
*   **Organisation of Data:** Arranging data in a meaningful way (e.g., ascending/descending order, frequency distribution table).
    *   **Frequency:** The number of times a particular observation occurs.
*   **Measures of Central Tendency:** Used to find a "typical" or "average" value in a dataset.
    *   **Arithmetic Mean (Average):** Sum of all observations / Number of observations.
    *   **Median:** The middle value of a dataset when arranged in ascending or descending order.
        *   If 'n' (number of observations) is odd, Median = ((n+1)/2)th observation.
        *   If 'n' is even, Median = Average of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th observations.
    *   **Mode:** The observation that occurs most frequently in the dataset. A dataset can have one mode (unimodal), more than one mode (multimodal), or no mode.
*   **Range:** Difference between the highest and lowest observation in a dataset.
*   **Bar Graph:** A visual representation of data using bars of uniform width, whose heights are proportional to the values they represent.
    *   **Double Bar Graph:** Used to compare two sets of data simultaneously.
*   **Probability (Basic Concept):** A measure of the likelihood of an event occurring.
    *   Probability = (Number of Favorable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Outcomes)
    *   Probability ranges from 0 (impossible event) to 1 (certain event).

### Chapter 4: Simple Equations

*   **Variable:** A symbol (usually a letter like x, y, z) that can take various numerical values.
*   **Constant:** A fixed numerical value.
*   **Algebraic Expression:** A combination of variables and constants connected by mathematical operations (+, -, ×, ÷). (e.g., 2x + 5)
*   **Equation:** A statement of equality between two algebraic expressions, involving one or more variables. (e.g., 2x + 5 = 11)
*   **Solving an Equation:** Finding the value(s) of the variable(s) that make the equation true.
    *   **Balancing Method:** Perform the same operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide) on both sides of the equation to isolate the variable.
    *   **Transposing Method:** Move terms from one side of the equation to the other by changing their sign. (e.g., if +5 moves to the other side, it becomes -5).
*   **Forming Equations:** Translate word problems into mathematical equations.

### Chapter 5: Lines and Angles

*   **Basic Geometric Ideas:**
    *   **Point:** A location.
    *   **Line:** A straight path extending infinitely in both directions.
    *   **Line Segment:** A part of a line with two endpoints.
    *   **Ray:** A part of a line with one endpoint and extending infinitely in one direction.
*   **Angles:** Formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex).
    *   **Vertex:** The common endpoint.
    *   **Arms:** The two rays.
    *   **Types of Angles:**
        *   **Acute Angle:** < 90°
        *   **Right Angle:** = 90°
        *   **Obtuse Angle:** > 90° and < 180°
        *   **Straight Angle:** = 180°
        *   **Reflex Angle:** > 180° and < 360°
        *   **Complete Angle:** = 360°
*   **Pairs of Angles:**
    *   **Complementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 90°.
    *   **Supplementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 180°.
    *   **Adjacent Angles:** Have a common vertex, a common arm, and non-common arms are on opposite sides of the common arm.
    *   **Linear Pair:** Adjacent angles that are supplementary (form a straight line).
    *   **Vertically Opposite Angles:** Formed when two lines intersect. They are equal.
*   **Transversal:** A line that intersects two or more lines at distinct points.
*   **Angles formed by a Transversal with Parallel Lines:**
    *   **Corresponding Angles:** Angles in the same relative position at each intersection. They are equal. (e.g., ∠1 = ∠5, ∠2 = ∠6, etc.)
    *   **Alternate Interior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and between the parallel lines. They are equal. (e.g., ∠3 = ∠6, ∠4 = ∠5)
    *   **Alternate Exterior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and outside the parallel lines. They are equal. (e.g., ∠1 = ∠8, ∠2 = ∠7)
    *   **Interior Angles on the Same Side of Transversal (Consecutive Interior Angles):** Angles between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal. They are supplementary (sum is 180°). (e.g., ∠3 + ∠5 = 180°, ∠4 + ∠6 = 180°)

### Chapter 6: The Triangle and its Properties

*   **Triangle:** A simple closed curve made of three line segments.
    *   Has 3 vertices, 3 sides, and 3 angles.
*   **Classification by Sides:**
    *   **Scalene Triangle:** All three sides are of different lengths.
    *   **Isosceles Triangle:** Two sides are of equal length.
    *   **Equilateral Triangle:** All three sides are of equal length. (All angles are 60°).
*   **Classification by Angles:**
    *   **Acute-angled Triangle:** All three angles are acute (< 90°).
    *   **Right-angled Triangle:** One angle is a right angle (= 90°).
    *   **Obtuse-angled Triangle:** One angle is an obtuse angle (> 90°).
*   **Important Properties:**
    *   **Angle Sum Property:** The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is always 180°. (∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°)
    *   **Exterior Angle Property:** The measure of an exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles.
    *   **Triangle Inequality Property:** The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is always greater than the length of the third side. (a + b > c, b + c > a, a + c > b)
*   **Special Lines in a Triangle:**
    *   **Median:** A line segment joining a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. A triangle has 3 medians.
    *   **Altitude:** A perpendicular line segment from a vertex to the opposite side. A triangle has 3 altitudes.
*   **Pythagoras Property (for Right-angled Triangles):** In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (legs).
    *   Hypotenuse² = Leg₁² + Leg₂² (or c² = a² + b²)

### Chapter 7: Congruence of Triangles

*   **Congruence:** Two figures are congruent if they have exactly the same shape and same size. (Symbol: ≅)
    *   For line segments: Same length.
    *   For angles: Same measure.
*   **Congruence of Triangles:** Two triangles are congruent if all corresponding sides and all corresponding angles are equal.
*   **Criteria for Congruence:**
    *   **SSS (Side-Side-Side):** If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
    *   **SAS (Side-Angle-Side):** If two sides and the included angle (the angle between those two sides) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
    *   **ASA (Angle-Side-Angle):** If two angles and the included side of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
    *   **RHS (Right Angle-Hypotenuse-Side):** If, in two right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
*   **CPCTC:** Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent. Once two triangles are proven congruent by one of the criteria, then all their corresponding parts (sides and angles) are equal.

### Chapter 8: Comparing Quantities

*   **Ratio:** Comparison of two quantities of the same unit by division. (e.g., 2:3 or 2/3). Ratios must be in their simplest form.
*   **Proportion:** An equality of two ratios. (a:b :: c:d or a/b = c/d).
    *   Product of Extremes = Product of Means (a × d = b × c).
*   **Percentage:** "Per hundred" or "out of one hundred." Denoted by %.
    *   To convert a fraction/decimal to percentage: Multiply by 100%. (e.g., 1/2 = 0.5 = 0.5 × 100% = 50%)
    *   To convert a percentage to fraction/decimal: Divide by 100. (e.g., 25% = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25)
*   **Profit and Loss:**
    *   **Cost Price (CP):** The price at which an item is bought.
    *   **Selling Price (SP):** The price at which an item is sold.
    *   **Profit:** If SP > CP. Profit = SP - CP.
    *   **Loss:** If CP > SP. Loss = CP - SP.
    *   **Profit %:** (Profit / CP) × 100%
    *   **Loss %:** (Loss / CP) × 100%
*   **Simple Interest:** Interest calculated only on the principal amount.
    *   **Principal (P):** The initial amount of money deposited or borrowed.
    *   **Rate (R):** The interest rate per annum (per year).
    *   **Time (T):** The period for which the money is deposited or borrowed (in years).
    *   **Simple Interest (I):** (P × R × T) / 100
    *   **Amount (A):** The total money paid back or received at the end of the time period. A = P + I.

### Chapter 9: Rational Numbers

*   **Definition:** A number that can be expressed in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
    *   All integers, fractions, and terminating/repeating decimals are rational numbers.
*   **Standard Form (Simplest Form):** A rational number p/q is in standard form if q is a positive integer and there are no common factors between p and q other than 1.
*   **Equivalent Rational Numbers:** Obtained by multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer.
*   **Rational Numbers on a Number Line:** Can be represented similarly to fractions.
*   **Comparison of Rational Numbers:**
    *   With same denominator: Compare numerators.
    *   With different denominators: Find a common denominator (LCM) and then compare numerators.
    *   Every positive rational number is greater than 0 and every negative rational number.
    *   Every negative rational number is less than 0 and every positive rational number.
*   **Operations:** Same rules as for fractions, extended to include negative numbers.
    *   **Addition/Subtraction:** Find a common denominator.
    *   **Multiplication:** (Product of numerators) / (Product of denominators).
    *   **Division:** Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
*   **Rational Numbers Between Two Rational Numbers:** There are infinitely many rational numbers between any two given rational numbers. (e.g., find common denominator, then scale up if needed).

### Chapter 10: Practical Geometry (Constructions)

*   **Tools:** Ruler (straightedge), Compass.
*   **Construction of Parallel Lines:**
    *   Using transversal and corresponding angles (or alternate interior angles).
*   **Construction of Triangles:** Requires three measurements, under certain conditions.
    *   **SSS Criterion:** When the lengths of all three sides are given.
    *   **SAS Criterion:** When the lengths of two sides and the measure of the included angle are given.
    *   **ASA Criterion:** When the measures of two angles and the length of the included side are given.
    *   **RHS Criterion:** When the length of the hypotenuse and one leg of a right-angled triangle are given.
    *   *Note:* It's not always possible to construct a unique triangle with any three given measurements (e.g., angle sum property, triangle inequality property must hold).

### Chapter 11: Perimeter and Area

*   **Perimeter:** The total distance around the boundary of a 2D closed figure. Measured in units of length (cm, m, km).
*   **Area:** The amount of surface enclosed by a 2D closed figure. Measured in square units (cm², m², km²).
*   **Formulas:**
    *   **Square:**
        *   Perimeter = 4 × side (s)
        *   Area = side × side = s²
    *   **Rectangle:**
        *   Perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth) = 2(l + b)
        *   Area = length × breadth = l × b
    *   **Parallelogram:**
        *   Area = base × height = b × h
    *   **Triangle:**
        *   Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × b × h
    *   **Circle:**
        *   **Circumference (Perimeter):** C = 2πr or C = πd (where r = radius, d = diameter, π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14)
        *   **Area:** A = πr²
*   **Conversion of Units:**
    *   1 cm² = 100 mm²
    *   1 m² = 10000 cm²
    *   1 hectare = 10000 m²
    *   1 km² = 100 hectares

### Chapter 12: Algebraic Expressions

*   **Term:** Parts of an expression separated by + or - signs. (e.g., in 2x + 5, 2x and 5 are terms).
*   **Factors:** When a term is expressed as a product of its variables and constants. (e.g., factors of 2x are 2 and x).
*   **Coefficient:** The numerical factor of a term. (e.g., in 5x, 5 is the coefficient; in -x², -1 is the coefficient).
*   **Like Terms:** Terms having the same algebraic factors (same variables raised to the same powers). (e.g., 2x and -5x; 3xy² and 7xy²).
*   **Unlike Terms:** Terms with different algebraic factors. (e.g., 2x and 3y; 2x² and 3x).
*   **Types of Expressions:**
    *   **Monomial:** An expression with only one term. (e.g., 5x)
    *   **Binomial:** An expression with two unlike terms. (e.g., 2x + 3)
    *   **Trinomial:** An expression with three unlike terms. (e.g., x² + 2x - 1)
    *   **Polynomial:** An expression with one or more terms (general term).
*   **Operations on Algebraic Expressions:**
    *   **Addition/Subtraction:** Combine like terms by adding/subtracting their coefficients. Unlike terms cannot be combined.
*   **Value of an Expression:** Substitute the given numerical values for the variables into the expression and simplify.

### Chapter 13: Exponents and Powers

*   **Base and Exponent:** In a^n, 'a' is the base and 'n' is the exponent (or power). It means 'a' is multiplied by itself 'n' times. (e.g., 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2).
*   **Laws of Exponents:**
    *   **Multiplication:** a^m × a^n = a^(m+n) (When bases are same, add powers)
    *   **Division:** a^m ÷ a^n = a^(m-n) (When bases are same, subtract powers)
    *   **Power of a Power:** (a^m)^n = a^(m×n)
    *   **Power of a Product:** (ab)^m = a^m × b^m
    *   **Power of a Quotient:** (a/b)^m = a^m / b^m
    *   **Zero Exponent:** a^0 = 1 (Any non-zero number raised to the power 0 is 1)
    *   **One Exponent:** a^1 = a
*   **Standard Form (Scientific Notation):** A way of writing very large or very small numbers.
    *   A number is expressed as k × 10^n, where 1 ≤ k < 10 and n is an integer.
    *   (e.g., 3,450,000 = 3.45 × 10⁶; 0.0000021 = 2.1 × 10⁻⁶)

### Chapter 14: Symmetry

*   **Symmetry:** When a figure or object can be divided into two or more parts that are identical in shape and size.
*   **Line Symmetry (Reflectional Symmetry):** A figure has line symmetry if it can be folded along a line such that one half exactly superimposes the other half. The fold line is called the **axis of symmetry** or **line of symmetry**.
    *   Shapes can have 0, 1, 2, or more axes of symmetry (e.g., square has 4, rectangle has 2, circle has infinite).
*   **Rotational Symmetry:** A figure has rotational symmetry if it looks exactly the same after being rotated by a partial turn (less than 360°) about a fixed point (center of rotation).
    *   **Order of Rotational Symmetry:** The number of times a figure looks identical during a full 360° rotation.
    *   **Angle of Rotational Symmetry:** The smallest angle by which the figure must be rotated to look exactly the same. (Angle = 360° / Order).

### Chapter 15: Visualizing Solid Shapes

*   **2D Shapes:** Plane figures that have only length and breadth (e.g., square, circle, triangle).
*   **3D Shapes (Solid Shapes):** Figures that have length, breadth, and height (or depth). They occupy space. (e.g., cube, cuboid, sphere, cylinder, cone, pyramid).
*   **Key Terms for 3D Shapes:**
    *   **Faces:** The flat surfaces of a solid shape.
    *   **Edges:** The line segments where two faces meet.
    *   **Vertices:** The corners where three or more edges meet.
*   **Nets for 3D Shapes:** A 2D pattern that can be folded to form a 3D solid shape. (e.g., net of a cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, pyramid).
*   **Drawing 3D Shapes:**
    *   **Oblique Sketches:** Do not have proportional lengths but give a good visual.
    *   **Isometric Sketches:** Are drawn on isometric dot paper, with proportional lengths.
*   **Views of 3D Shapes:** Observing a 3D object from different perspectives.
    *   **Front View:** What you see from the front.
    *   **Side View:** What you see from the side.
    *   **Top View:** What you see from above.

**General Tips for 7th Standard Maths:**

*   **Practice Regularly:** Maths is all about practice. Work through examples and exercises.
*   **Understand Concepts:** Don't just memorize formulas. Understand *why* a formula works or *what* a concept means.
*   **Review Previous Concepts:** 7th standard maths builds on 6th. Make sure your basics are strong.
*   **Pay Attention to Signs:** Especially with integers, one wrong sign can lead to a completely wrong answer.
*   **Show All Steps:** This helps in identifying errors and getting partial credit even if the final answer is wrong.
*   **Units:** Always write appropriate units (cm, m², %, etc.) in your answers.
*   **Draw Diagrams:** For geometry problems, drawing a clear diagram can help visualize the problem and identify relevant properties.
