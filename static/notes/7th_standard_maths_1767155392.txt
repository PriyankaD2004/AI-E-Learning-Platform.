## Detailed Notes: 7th Standard Mathematics

### Introduction to 7th Grade Maths

7th Standard Maths builds upon the foundational concepts learned in earlier grades and introduces new, more complex topics in algebra, geometry, and data handling. The focus shifts towards understanding 'why' certain rules work and applying them to solve real-world problems.

### Chapter 1: Integers

**Introduction:** Integers are a set of whole numbers including positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.
*   **Positive Integers:** 1, 2, 3, ...
*   **Negative Integers:** -1, -2, -3, ...
*   **Zero:** 0 (neither positive nor negative)

**1.1 Operations on Integers:**

*   **Addition:**
    *   **Same Signs:** Add the absolute values and keep the common sign.
        *   *Example:* 5 + 3 = 8; (-5) + (-3) = -8
    *   **Different Signs:** Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value, and take the sign of the integer with the larger absolute value.
        *   *Example:* 5 + (-3) = 2; (-5) + 3 = -2
*   **Subtraction:** Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its additive inverse (opposite).
    *   *Rule:* a - b = a + (-b)
    *   *Example:* 5 - 3 = 5 + (-3) = 2; 3 - 5 = 3 + (-5) = -2; 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8
*   **Multiplication:**
    *   **Same Signs:** Product is positive.
        *   *Example:* 5 × 3 = 15; (-5) × (-3) = 15
    *   **Different Signs:** Product is negative.
        *   *Example:* 5 × (-3) = -15; (-5) × 3 = -15
*   **Division:** Follows the same sign rules as multiplication.
    *   **Same Signs:** Quotient is positive.
        *   *Example:* 15 ÷ 3 = 5; (-15) ÷ (-3) = 5
    *   **Different Signs:** Quotient is negative.
        *   *Example:* 15 ÷ (-3) = -5; (-15) ÷ 3 = -5

**1.2 Properties of Integers:**
*   **Closure Property:** Integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication (e.g., sum/difference/product of two integers is an integer). Not closed under division.
*   **Commutative Property:**
    *   Addition: a + b = b + a
    *   Multiplication: a × b = b × a
    *   *Not* for subtraction or division.
*   **Associative Property:**
    *   Addition: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
    *   Multiplication: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
    *   *Not* for subtraction or division.
*   **Distributive Property:** a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
*   **Additive Identity:** a + 0 = a
*   **Multiplicative Identity:** a × 1 = a
*   **Additive Inverse:** a + (-a) = 0

### Chapter 2: Fractions and Decimals

**Introduction:** Fractions represent parts of a whole. Decimals are another way to represent fractions, especially those with denominators of 10, 100, 1000, etc.

**2.1 Fractions:**
*   **Types:**
    *   **Proper Fraction:** Numerator < Denominator (e.g., 2/3)
    *   **Improper Fraction:** Numerator ≥ Denominator (e.g., 5/3)
    *   **Mixed Fraction:** Whole number + Proper fraction (e.g., 1 2/3)
    *   **Equivalent Fractions:** Represent the same value (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6)
*   **Multiplication of Fractions:** (Numerator × Numerator) / (Denominator × Denominator)
    *   *Example:* (2/3) × (4/5) = (2×4) / (3×5) = 8/15
    *   "of" means multiply (e.g., 1/2 of 10 = 1/2 × 10 = 5)
*   **Division of Fractions:** Multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
    *   *Rule:* (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b) × (d/c)
    *   *Example:* (2/3) ÷ (4/5) = (2/3) × (5/4) = 10/12 = 5/6

**2.2 Decimals:**
*   **Place Value:** Tenths, hundredths, thousandths, etc. (e.g., 0.1, 0.01, 0.001)
*   **Multiplication of Decimals:**
    *   Multiply as whole numbers.
    *   Count the total number of decimal places in the factors.
    *   Place the decimal point in the product so it has the same total number of decimal places.
    *   *Example:* 2.5 × 1.3
        *   25 × 13 = 325
        *   Total decimal places = 1 (in 2.5) + 1 (in 1.3) = 2
        *   Result: 3.25
*   **Division of Decimals:**
    *   If the divisor is a decimal, convert it to a whole number by multiplying both divisor and dividend by 10, 100, etc.
    *   Then perform division as with whole numbers.
    *   *Example:* 1.25 ÷ 0.5
        *   Multiply both by 10: 12.5 ÷ 5
        *   12.5 ÷ 5 = 2.5

### Chapter 3: Data Handling

**Introduction:** Data handling is the process of collecting, organizing, representing, and interpreting data.

**3.1 Collecting and Organizing Data:**
*   **Raw Data:** Data collected in its original form.
*   **Frequency:** The number of times a particular observation occurs.
*   **Frequency Distribution Table:** Organizes data by showing the frequency of each item.
*   **Range:** The difference between the highest and lowest observation.

**3.2 Measures of Central Tendency:**
*   **Mean (Average):** Sum of all observations / Number of observations
    *   *Example:* For data {2, 3, 7, 8, 5}, Mean = (2+3+7+8+5) / 5 = 25 / 5 = 5
*   **Median:** The middle value of the data when arranged in ascending or descending order.
    *   If n (number of observations) is odd, Median = (n+1)/2 th term.
    *   If n is even, Median = Average of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th terms.
    *   *Example (odd):* {2, 3, 5, 7, 8}, Median = 5 (3rd term)
    *   *Example (even):* {2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9}, Median = (5+7)/2 = 6 (average of 3rd and 4th)
*   **Mode:** The observation that occurs most frequently. A data set can have one mode (unimodal), multiple modes (multimodal), or no mode.
    *   *Example:* {2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 3, 8}, Mode = 3

**3.3 Bar Graphs and Double Bar Graphs:**
*   **Bar Graph:** Uses bars of uniform width, whose heights are proportional to the values they represent. Used for comparing different categories.
*   **Double Bar Graph:** Used to compare two sets of data simultaneously.

**3.4 Probability (Introduction):**
*   **Chance:** The possibility of an event happening.
*   **Outcome:** A possible result of an experiment.
*   **Event:** One or more outcomes of an experiment.
*   **Probability:** (Number of favorable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes)
    *   Probability ranges from 0 (impossible event) to 1 (certain event).
    *   *Example:* Rolling a fair die.
        *   Total outcomes = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} (6 outcomes)
        *   Event: Getting an even number = {2, 4, 6} (3 favorable outcomes)
        *   Probability of getting an even number = 3/6 = 1/2

### Chapter 4: Simple Equations

**Introduction:** An equation is a statement of equality between two algebraic expressions, involving one or more variables. Simple equations usually have one variable with a power of 1.

**4.1 What is an Equation?**
*   It has an equals sign (=).
*   It has a Left Hand Side (LHS) and a Right Hand Side (RHS).
*   It contains variables (letters like x, y, z) and constants (numbers).
*   *Example:* x + 5 = 10 (x is the variable, 5 and 10 are constants)

**4.2 Solving Simple Equations:**
*   The goal is to find the value of the variable that makes the equation true.
*   **Balancing Method (Transposition):**
    *   Add or subtract the same number from both sides.
    *   Multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero number.
    *   When moving a term from one side to the other, change its sign.
*   *Example 1 (Addition/Subtraction):*
    *   x + 5 = 12
    *   x = 12 - 5 (transpose +5 to -5)
    *   x = 7
*   *Example 2 (Multiplication/Division):*
    *   3x = 15
    *   x = 15 / 3 (transpose ×3 to ÷3)
    *   x = 5
*   *Example 3 (Two-step equation):*
    *   2x - 3 = 7
    *   2x = 7 + 3
    *   2x = 10
    *   x = 10 / 2
    *   x = 5

**4.3 Applications of Simple Equations (Word Problems):**
*   Read the problem carefully.
*   Identify the unknown quantity and represent it with a variable (e.g., x).
*   Translate the problem into an algebraic equation.
*   Solve the equation.
*   Check your answer in the original problem.
    *   *Example:* "If you add 7 to twice a number, you get 19. Find the number."
        *   Let the number be x.
        *   Twice the number = 2x
        *   Adding 7 to it = 2x + 7
        *   Equation: 2x + 7 = 19
        *   2x = 19 - 7
        *   2x = 12
        *   x = 6
        *   The number is 6.

### Chapter 5: Lines and Angles

**Introduction:** This chapter deals with basic geometric figures like lines, rays, segments, and various types of angles and their relationships.

**5.1 Basic Geometric Terms:**
*   **Point:** A location in space (no size).
*   **Line:** A straight path extending infinitely in both directions (no endpoints).
*   **Line Segment:** A part of a line with two distinct endpoints.
*   **Ray:** A part of a line with one endpoint, extending infinitely in one direction.
*   **Angle:** Formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex).

**5.2 Types of Angles:**
*   **Acute Angle:** Between 0° and 90°.
*   **Right Angle:** Exactly 90°.
*   **Obtuse Angle:** Between 90° and 180°.
*   **Straight Angle:** Exactly 180°.
*   **Reflex Angle:** Between 180° and 360°.
*   **Complementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 90°.
*   **Supplementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 180°.
*   **Adjacent Angles:** Share a common vertex and a common arm, but no common interior points.
*   **Linear Pair:** Adjacent angles that are supplementary (form a straight line).
*   **Vertically Opposite Angles:** Formed when two lines intersect. They are equal.

**5.3 Pairs of Lines:**
*   **Intersecting Lines:** Lines that cross each other at a common point.
*   **Parallel Lines:** Lines that never intersect, maintaining a constant distance apart.
*   **Transversal:** A line that intersects two or more other lines (usually parallel lines) at distinct points.

**5.4 Angles Formed by a Transversal with Parallel Lines:**
When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, several pairs of angles are formed, and they have specific relationships:
*   **Corresponding Angles:** Angles in the same relative position at each intersection. They are equal.
    *   *Example:* Angle 1 and Angle 5 are corresponding angles.
*   **Alternate Interior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and between the parallel lines. They are equal.
    *   *Example:* Angle 3 and Angle 6 are alternate interior angles.
*   **Alternate Exterior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and outside the parallel lines. They are equal.
    *   *Example:* Angle 1 and Angle 8 are alternate exterior angles.
*   **Interior Angles on the Same Side of the Transversal (Consecutive Interior Angles / Co-interior Angles):** Angles between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal. They are supplementary (sum up to 180°).
    *   *Example:* Angle 3 and Angle 5 are co-interior angles.

### Chapter 6: The Triangle and its Properties

**Introduction:** A triangle is a polygon with three sides and three angles. It's a fundamental shape in geometry.

**6.1 Classification of Triangles:**
*   **By Sides:**
    *   **Scalene Triangle:** All three sides are of different lengths.
    *   **Isosceles Triangle:** Two sides are of equal length.
    *   **Equilateral Triangle:** All three sides are of equal length (and all angles are 60°).
*   **By Angles:**
    *   **Acute-angled Triangle:** All three angles are acute (less than 90°).
    *   **Right-angled Triangle:** One angle is a right angle (exactly 90°).
    *   **Obtuse-angled Triangle:** One angle is an obtuse angle (greater than 90°).

**6.2 Properties of Triangles:**
*   **Angle Sum Property:** The sum of the interior angles of any triangle is always 180°. (∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°)
*   **Exterior Angle Property:** An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles.
    *   *Example:* If angle D is an exterior angle at vertex C, then ∠D = ∠A + ∠B.

**6.3 Medians and Altitudes:**
*   **Median:** A line segment joining a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. A triangle has three medians.
*   **Altitude:** A perpendicular line segment from a vertex to the opposite side. A triangle has three altitudes.

**6.4 Triangle Inequality Property:**
*   The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is always greater than the length of the third side.
    *   a + b > c; b + c > a; a + c > b

**6.5 Pythagoras Property (for Right-angled Triangles only):**
*   In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (legs).
    *   **Hypotenuse² = Leg1² + Leg2²** (or c² = a² + b²)

### Chapter 7: Congruence of Triangles

**Introduction:** Congruence means "exactly the same shape and size." If two figures are congruent, one can be placed exactly over the other to cover it completely.

**7.1 Congruent Figures:**
*   Two line segments are congruent if they have the same length.
*   Two angles are congruent if they have the same measure.
*   Two circles are congruent if they have the same radius.
*   Two squares are congruent if they have the same side length.

**7.2 Congruence of Triangles:**
Two triangles are congruent if their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal.
We use specific criteria to prove congruence:

*   **SSS (Side-Side-Side) Congruence Rule:** If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
*   **SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Congruence Rule:** If two sides and the *included* angle (the angle between those two sides) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
*   **ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Congruence Rule:** If two angles and the *included* side (the side between those two angles) of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.
*   **RHS (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side) Congruence Rule:** If in two right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

### Chapter 8: Comparing Quantities

**Introduction:** This chapter deals with comparing different quantities using ratios, percentages, and applying these concepts to profit/loss and simple interest.

**8.1 Ratio and Proportion:**
*   **Ratio:** Compares two quantities of the same unit. Expressed as a:b or a/b.
    *   *Example:* Ratio of 20g to 40g is 20:40 or 1:2.
*   **Equivalent Ratios:** Ratios that represent the same relationship (e.g., 1:2 is equivalent to 2:4).
*   **Proportion:** An equality of two ratios. If a:b = c:d, then a, b, c, d are in proportion.
    *   Product of extremes (a×d) = Product of means (b×c).

**8.2 Percentages:**
*   **Percent (%):** Means "per hundred" or "out of 100".
*   **Converting Fractions/Decimals to Percent:**
    *   Fraction to Percent: Multiply by 100%. (e.g., 1/4 = 1/4 × 100% = 25%)
    *   Decimal to Percent: Multiply by 100%. (e.g., 0.25 = 0.25 × 100% = 25%)
*   **Converting Percent to Fractions/Decimals:**
    *   Percent to Fraction: Divide by 100. (e.g., 25% = 25/100 = 1/4)
    *   Percent to Decimal: Divide by 100. (e.g., 25% = 25/100 = 0.25)
*   **Finding a Percentage of a Quantity:** Multiply the quantity by the percentage (expressed as a fraction or decimal).
    *   *Example:* 20% of 50 = (20/100) × 50 = 10.
*   **Percentage Increase/Decrease:** [(Change in quantity) / (Original quantity)] × 100%

**8.3 Profit and Loss:**
*   **Cost Price (CP):** The price at which an item is bought.
*   **Selling Price (SP):** The price at which an item is sold.
*   **Profit:** If SP > CP. Profit = SP - CP.
*   **Loss:** If CP > SP. Loss = CP - SP.
*   **Profit Percentage (P%):** (Profit / CP) × 100%
*   **Loss Percentage (L%):** (Loss / CP) × 100%

**8.4 Simple Interest:**
*   **Principal (P):** The initial amount of money.
*   **Rate (R):** The percentage at which interest is charged per annum.
*   **Time (T):** The duration for which the money is borrowed/lent (in years).
*   **Simple Interest (SI):** The interest calculated only on the principal amount.
    *   **SI = (P × R × T) / 100**
*   **Amount (A):** The total money to be paid back (Principal + Interest).
    *   **A = P + SI**

### Chapter 9: Rational Numbers

**Introduction:** Rational numbers include all integers and fractions. They are numbers that can be expressed in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

**9.1 Definition and Standard Form:**
*   **Rational Number:** Any number that can be written as a fraction p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not zero.
    *   *Examples:* 2/3, -5/7, 4 (as 4/1), 0 (as 0/1)
*   **Standard Form:** A rational number is in standard form if its denominator is a positive integer and the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1 (i.e., they are co-prime).
    *   *Example:* -2/3 is in standard form. 4/6 is not; its standard form is 2/3.
*   **Equivalent Rational Numbers:** Represent the same value (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4 = -3/-6).

**9.2 Operations on Rational Numbers:**
*   **Addition/Subtraction:**
    *   With same denominators: Add/subtract numerators.
    *   With different denominators: Find LCM, convert to equivalent fractions, then add/subtract numerators.
    *   *Example:* (1/3) + (1/4) = (4/12) + (3/12) = 7/12
*   **Multiplication:** (Numerator × Numerator) / (Denominator × Denominator)
    *   *Example:* (-2/3) × (4/5) = -8/15
*   **Division:** Multiply the first rational number by the reciprocal of the second rational number.
    *   *Example:* (1/2) ÷ (3/4) = (1/2) × (4/3) = 4/6 = 2/3

**9.3 Representation on the Number Line:**
*   Rational numbers can be represented on a number line. Positive rational numbers are to the right of zero, negative to the left.
    *   *Example:* To represent 1/2, divide the segment between 0 and 1 into two equal parts.

**9.4 Comparison of Rational Numbers:**
*   To compare, convert them to equivalent rational numbers with a common denominator. Then compare their numerators.
    *   *Example:* Compare 2/3 and 3/4. LCM of 3,4 is 12.
        *   2/3 = 8/12
        *   3/4 = 9/12
        *   Since 9 > 8, 3/4 > 2/3.

### Chapter 10: Practical Geometry

**Introduction:** This chapter focuses on constructing various geometric shapes using basic tools like a ruler, compass, and protractor.

**10.1 Construction of Parallel Lines:**
*   **Method:** Using alternate interior angles or corresponding angles.
    *   Steps: Draw a line 'l' and a point 'P' not on 'l'. Draw a transversal line 'm' through P intersecting 'l' at Q. With Q as center, draw an arc intersecting 'l' and 'm'. With P as center and same radius, draw an arc intersecting 'm'. Measure the angle formed by the transversal and 'l'. With the same measure, mark an angle at P on the new arc. Draw a line through P and this mark. This line will be parallel to 'l'.

**10.2 Construction of Triangles (using SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS criteria):**

*   **SSS Construction:** Given the lengths of all three sides.
    *   Steps: Draw one side as a base. With one endpoint as center and second side length as radius, draw an arc. With the other endpoint as center and third side length as radius, draw another arc. The intersection point is the third vertex. Join the vertices.
*   **SAS Construction:** Given two sides and the included angle.
    *   Steps: Draw one side as base. At one endpoint, construct the given included angle. Along the ray of the angle, mark the length of the second given side. Join the endpoints.
*   **ASA Construction:** Given two angles and the included side.
    *   Steps: Draw the included side as base. At each endpoint, construct the given angles. The intersection of the rays of these angles is the third vertex.
*   **RHS Construction (for Right-angled Triangles):** Given the hypotenuse and one leg.
    *   Steps: Draw the given leg as base. At one endpoint of the leg, construct a 90° angle. With the other endpoint as center and the hypotenuse length as radius, draw an arc intersecting the ray of the 90° angle. This intersection is the third vertex.

### Chapter 11: Perimeter and Area

**Introduction:** Perimeter is the distance around a 2D shape, and area is the amount of surface a 2D shape covers.

**11.1 Perimeter:**
*   **Square:** 4 × side
*   **Rectangle:** 2 × (length + breadth)
*   **Triangle:** Sum of all three sides
*   **Circle (Circumference):** 2πr or πd (where r = radius, d = diameter, π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14)

**11.2 Area:**
*   **Square:** side × side (s²)
*   **Rectangle:** length × breadth (l × b)
*   **Triangle:** (1/2) × base × height (1/2 b h)
*   **Parallelogram:** base × height (b × h)
*   **Circle:** πr²

**11.3 Conversion of Units:**
*   **Length:** 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm
*   **Area:** 1 m² = 10000 cm², 1 hectare = 10000 m²

### Chapter 12: Algebraic Expressions

**Introduction:** Algebraic expressions are combinations of variables, constants, and mathematical operations (+, -, ×, ÷).

**12.1 Terms of an Algebraic Expression:**
*   **Variable:** A symbol (usually a letter) that represents an unknown value (e.g., x, y, a).
*   **Constant:** A fixed numerical value (e.g., 5, -7, 100).
*   **Coefficient:** The numerical factor of a term with variables (e.g., in 5x, 5 is the coefficient).
*   **Term:** A product of factors. In an expression, terms are separated by + or - signs.
    *   *Example:* In 5x + 3y - 7, the terms are 5x, 3y, and -7.

**12.2 Types of Algebraic Expressions:**
*   **Monomial:** An expression with one term (e.g., 5x, 7y²)
*   **Binomial:** An expression with two terms (e.g., x + y, 2a - 3b)
*   **Trinomial:** An expression with three terms (e.g., x² + 2x + 1)
*   **Polynomial:** An expression with one or more terms (includes monomial, binomial, trinomial).

**12.3 Like and Unlike Terms:**
*   **Like Terms:** Terms that have the same variables raised to the same powers (e.g., 5x and -2x; 3y² and 7y²).
*   **Unlike Terms:** Terms that have different variables or different powers of the same variable (e.g., 5x and 3y; 2x and 2x²).

**12.4 Addition and Subtraction of Algebraic Expressions:**
*   Only like terms can be added or subtracted.
*   Add/subtract their coefficients, keeping the variable part the same.
    *   *Example:* (5x + 3y) + (2x - y) = (5x + 2x) + (3y - y) = 7x + 2y
    *   *Example:* (7a - 4b) - (3a - 2b) = 7a - 4b - 3a + 2b = (7a - 3a) + (-4b + 2b) = 4a - 2b

**12.5 Value of an Expression:**
*   To find the value of an expression, substitute the given numerical values for the variables and then simplify.
    *   *Example:* If x=2, find the value of 3x + 5.
        *   3(2) + 5 = 6 + 5 = 11

### Chapter 13: Exponents and Powers

**Introduction:** Exponents (or powers) are a way of expressing repeated multiplication of the same number.

**13.1 Basics of Exponents:**
*   **Base:** The number that is being multiplied.
*   **Exponent (or Power):** The small number written above and to the right of the base, indicating how many times the base is multiplied by itself.
*   *Example:* In 2⁵, 2 is the base, and 5 is the exponent. 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

**13.2 Laws of Exponents (for non-zero bases a, b and integers m, n):**
*   **Law 1: Product of Powers:** aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
    *   *Example:* 2³ × 2² = 2³⁺² = 2⁵
*   **Law 2: Quotient of Powers:** aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (if m > n)
    *   *Example:* 5⁵ ÷ 5² = 5⁵⁻² = 5³
*   **Law 3: Power of a Power:** (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
    *   *Example:* ( (3²)⁴ = 3²ˣ⁴ = 3⁸
*   **Law 4: Power of a Product:** (a × b)ᵐ = aᵐ × bᵐ
    *   *Example:* (2 × 3)⁴ = 2⁴ × 3⁴
*   **Law 5: Power of a Quotient:** (a / b)ᵐ = aᵐ / bᵐ
    *   *Example:* (2 / 5)³ = 2³ / 5³
*   **Law 6: Zero Exponent:** a⁰ = 1 (any non-zero number raised to the power 0 is 1)
    *   *Example:* 7⁰ = 1; (-3)⁰ = 1
*   **Law 7: Negative Exponents (sometimes introduced):** a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
    *   *Example:* 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

**13.3 Expressing Large Numbers in Standard Form (Scientific Notation):**
*   A number is in standard form if it is written as k × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ k < 10 and n is an integer.
    *   *Example:* 3,450,000 = 3.45 × 10⁶
    *   *Example:* 0.0000078 = 7.8 × 10⁻⁶

### Chapter 14: Symmetry

**Introduction:** Symmetry refers to the property of an object being invariant under certain transformations (like reflection or rotation).

**14.1 Line Symmetry (Reflectional Symmetry):**
*   A figure has line symmetry if it can be folded along a line such that one half exactly superimposes the other half.
*   **Axis of Symmetry (or Line of Symmetry):** The line along which the figure is folded.
*   *Examples:*
    *   Square: 4 lines of symmetry
    *   Rectangle: 2 lines of symmetry
    *   Equilateral Triangle: 3 lines of symmetry
    *   Isosceles Triangle: 1 line of symmetry
    *   Circle: Infinite lines of symmetry

**14.2 Rotational Symmetry:**
*   A figure has rotational symmetry if it looks exactly the same after being rotated by some angle (less than 360°) about a fixed point (center of rotation).
*   **Center of Rotation:** The fixed point around which the figure rotates.
*   **Angle of Rotation:** The smallest angle through which the figure must be rotated to look exactly the same.
*   **Order of Rotational Symmetry:** The number of times a figure looks the same during a full 360° rotation. (Order = 360° / Angle of Rotation)
    *   *Examples:*
        *   Square: Center is intersection of diagonals. Angle of rotation = 90°. Order = 4.
        *   Equilateral Triangle: Center is centroid. Angle of rotation = 120°. Order = 3.
        *   Circle: Infinite order.
        *   Rectangle: Angle of rotation = 180°. Order = 2.

### Chapter 15: Visualizing Solid Shapes

**Introduction:** This chapter introduces 3D shapes, their components, and different ways to represent them.

**15.1 2D and 3D Shapes:**
*   **2D (Two-Dimensional) Shapes:** Flat shapes that have only length and width (e.g., square, circle, triangle).
*   **3D (Three-Dimensional) Shapes (Solid Shapes):** Objects that have length, width, and height/depth (e.g., cube, sphere, cylinder).

**15.2 Elements of 3D Shapes:**
*   **Faces:** The flat surfaces of a 3D shape.
*   **Edges:** The line segments where two faces meet.
*   **Vertices:** The corners where three or more edges meet.
    *   *Example (Cube):* 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices

**15.3 Common 3D Shapes:**
*   **Cube:** 6 square faces.
*   **Cuboid:** 6 rectangular faces.
*   **Cylinder:** Two circular bases and a curved surface.
*   **Cone:** One circular base and a curved surface tapering to a point (apex).
*   **Sphere:** A perfectly round 3D object.
*   **Pyramid:** A base (polygon) and triangular faces meeting at an apex.
*   **Prism:** Two identical parallel bases and rectangular faces connecting them.

**15.4 Nets for Building 3D Shapes:**
*   A **Net** is a 2D pattern that can be folded to form a 3D shape.
    *   *Example:* A net of a cube consists of 6 squares arranged in a way that they can be folded to form a cube.

**15.5 Drawing Solid Shapes:**
*   **Oblique Sketches:** Freehand sketches that show the general shape but not necessarily accurate proportions or angles.
*   **Isometric Sketches:** Sketches drawn on isometric dot paper, which allow for accurate representation of lengths but angles are typically 30°, 90°, 150°.
*   **Views of 3D Objects:**
    *   **Front View:** What you see when looking from the front.
    *   **Side View:** What you see when looking from a side.
    *   **Top View:** What you see when looking from directly above.

### General Tips for Studying 7th Standard Maths:

1.  **Understand Concepts:** Don't just memorize formulas. Understand *why* they work.
2.  **Practice Regularly:** Maths is a skill; regular practice is key. Solve a variety of problems.
3.  **Clear Doubts:** Ask your teacher or friends if you don't understand something.
4.  **Review Previous Concepts:** 7th-grade math often builds on 6th-grade concepts.
5.  **Make a Formula Sheet:** Keep a concise list of important formulas and properties for quick revision.
6.  **Real-World Connections:** Try to relate math concepts to real-life situations to make them more interesting and easier to grasp.
